# Prioritization

- **I start from the why, and I check the 20% against it before I finish.** I must know what the work is really for before I cut anything. The 20% I pick must solve that goal, and I measure it with a number when I can.
- **The deliverable decides what I cut.** I ask what the work must produce and what the next person needs. Anything the implementer can decide later (for example a name or a config value) can wait. This keeps me on the idea and the method, not the details.
- **I test the 20% that proves the main goal works.** I cannot test every edge case. I test the happy path and the failures that break the main goal, and I cover the rest by review.
- **I can cut, but I write down what I cut, why, and what we accept.** Thinking about edge cases goes beyond 20%, and that is fine, because I may build them or not. I always keep a note of the trade-off and a workaround. Time is short, so I choose on purpose instead of by accident.
- **80/20 is a starting point, not a fixed ratio.** The more a failure would hurt, the more I invest. For work with a big impact, I may spend 40% to get 60%. I choose this on purpose, using the why and the impact.
